Did Congress Really Expose 'the Enterprise' in 2026, or Is a 1987 Term Being Recycled?

An unverified reading of the record suggests that Alex Krainer's claim of a 2026 congressional revelation of a parallel power structure may rest on the 1987 Iran-Contra reports and a journalist's essay, not on any identified 2026 congressional document.

Shadow Government Published November 22, 2026 · 7 sources

The Claim on the Table

Alex Krainer, writing on the Trendcompass Substack, asserts that "a full congressional investigation was launched and its proceedings revealed for the first time the existence of a parallel structure of power operating within legitimate government structures" [1]. According to the brief, the post says this network is also known as shadow government, deep state or the Enterprise, but it does not cite specific congressional hearing transcripts or reports to back that up [1]. The thesis here is narrow. Krainer's headline revelation rests on no congressional document that he identifies, and the documents that do use the term Enterprise come from 1987 and describe something smaller than what he claims [1][2][3].

What 'the Enterprise' Meant in 1987

The 1987 Report of the Congressional Committees Investigating the Iran-Contra Affair uses the Enterprise as a label for the National Security Council staff's covert operations [2]. The excerpts state that "the N.S.C. staff turned once again to the Enterprise" to carry out arms sales [2]. The full text adds that "the arms sales continued to generate funds for the Enterprise," with North and Poindexter named as advocates for continuing those sales [3]. On that wording, the Enterprise reads as an operational network built for a specific job, not a standing shadow state [2]. Possibility, not verified It is possible that Krainer's Enterprise claim is a misreading of these 1987 reports, where the term referred to a covert arms-smuggling network run by NSC staff rather than a structural parallel government, based on the report's use of the term to describe arms sales to Iran and the diversion of funds to the Contras [2][3].

The 2026 Record, or the Lack of One

According to the brief, searches of Congress.gov hearing transcripts and the Congressional Record for the 119th Congress turned up normal oversight activity and no mention of a shadow government or an Enterprise as a parallel power structure [4]. A 2025 Lead Stories fact-check found that, in a transcript of an April 7, 2025 Democratic shadow hearing, "no participant used that term," meaning shadow government [5]. The fact-check also said shadow hearing is a standard name for one-party committee meetings, not evidence of a shadow government [5]. Possibility, not verified It is possible that no 2026 congressional hearing transcript or report contains the term Enterprise in the structural sense Krainer claims, based on the 119th Congress transcript searches and the Lead Stories finding [4][5]. Possibility, not verified It is possible that Krainer's claim that the Enterprise came out on the record in 2026 is a misreading of the Iran-Contra reports, which were published in 1987, based on that report's narrow use of the term and the absence of any 2026 record using it in a structural sense [3][4].

The limits of this evidence matter. Proving a negative from a transcript index is hard, and the cited Congress.gov source is a listing page titled "House Hearing Transcripts," not a document that rules anything out [4]. The Lead Stories check covers one 2025 hearing and the phrase shadow government, not 2026 proceedings or the word Enterprise [5]. These sources make Krainer's claim look unsupported. They do not prove that no such record exists anywhere [5][4].

Moyers, Not Congress

The phrase secret government has a well-known source outside Congress. The Christian Science Monitor called Bill Moyers' 1987 work a "personal essay" released after the Iran-contra hearings had cooled [6]. Moyers introduced it himself as "this personal essay" in which "we'll look at that government in the shadows" [7]. Possibility, not verified It is possible that Krainer's reference to Moyers' Secret Government essay is being conflated with an official congressional report, when Moyers' work was journalism rather than a congressional investigation, based on the Monitor's description of it as a personal essay by a television newsman [6][7].

The Counterpoint, and Its Absence

The strongest case against this reading runs as follows. The 1987 reports describe a covert network operating outside legitimate institutions, so calling the Enterprise a parallel structure is a fair interpretation, even if no 2026 record repeats the term [2][3]. No source in this brief makes that case, and every source listed carries a supporting stance [1][2][3][5][6][7][4]. Even so, the argument deserves weight. A network that ran arms sales and diverted funds outside normal channels does fit some definitions of a shadow apparatus [2][3]. Where it falls short is on permanence and on dating. The quoted 1987 language describes a specific operation, and nothing in the brief ties it to any 2026 proceeding [2][3][4].

Nothing in the brief shows why Krainer framed his claim as he did, and this article makes no allegation about his motives [1]. What the brief does show is a gap. The record he points to is not among the documents surveyed, and the term he leans on has a documented 1987 meaning that is narrower than his claim [1][2][4].
