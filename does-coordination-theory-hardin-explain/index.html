Is the Three-Way Contest Between Coordination, Ideology, and Force a False Contest?

A possible misframing of Hume and Marx in the 2013 paper's theoretical framework may make coordination theory harder to refute than critics claim, but the brief could not fetch the primary texts and the only empirical anchor—a Federal Reserve speech—does not discriminate between the theories.

Coordination as Power Published November 22, 2026 · 9 sources

The Contest May Be Misframed

Wikipedia's collective-action article quotes Hume's 1738 Treatise: it is "impossible" for a thousand persons to agree because each "seeks a pretext to free himself of the trouble and expence" [1]. Wikipedia's false-consciousness article states that "The term was never used by Karl Marx" [2].

Possibility, not verified It is possible that the "Hume equals force" label is a misframing, because the passage Wikipedia quotes describes a coordination failure, not coercion, and this rests on the secondary quotation [1], not the primary text or the 2013 paper, which returned HTTP 403. Possibility, not verified It is possible that "ideology theory (Marx)" names a later construct, not Marx's own position, because Wikipedia says the term was never his and was theorized by later Marxists from the 1920s [2], unverified against his or Engels's writings.

What Barr's Speeches Actually Establish

Barr's 14 July 2026 speech says AI may "exacerbate inequality, eliminating some lower- and middle-income jobs while boosting the income and wealth of higher-income individuals" [3]; his 12 November speech reports the top 10 percent hold nearly three-fourths of wealth [4] and names three causes of declining labor share, including "the reduction in the bargaining power of workers" [5].

Possibility, not verified It is possible that this enumeration points to coordination failure rather than force, since Barr pairs bargaining-power loss with technological change without mentioning coercion, though he gives no reason for the decline, making it an economist's list, not a test of Hardin [5][3]. Possibility, not verified It is possible that new entrants, whom Barr says AI "might disproportionately affect," are least able to coordinate resistance, though no organizing data were found [6], and that displacement, which he lists first, best distinguishes the theories by dispersing workers [7].

The Fix Points Back to Collective Action

MIT Sloan authors argue that "AI needs a combination of new labor standards and tax reforms" [8]. Possibility, not verified It is possible that these institutional remedies require the kind of collective political action that coordination theory locates as the obstacle, because new labor standards and tax reforms cannot emerge without sustained, organized pressure from affected groups [8]. The article does not discuss Hardin or coordination theory, so the connection between the proposed fixes and the theoretical framework is the brief's inference, not the authors' own claim [8]. The MIT Sloan piece summarizes a working paper that was not read in full, leaving the scope, feasibility, and political pathway of the proposed remedies unverified [8].

The Case Against

The strongest objection is that the contest is confused before any empirical test. Wikipedia states that "The term was never used by Karl Marx," so "ideology theory (Marx)" names a later construct, not Marx's own position [2]. Barr's 14 July 2026 speech declines to forecast, saying "Since we don't know which of these futures will come about, it is useful to use potential scenarios" [9], leaving the case hypothetical and unable to discriminate between coordination failure, force, and ideology. No source in the brief supplies data on whether workers fail to organize, whether from fear of sanction, or whether AI gains in 2026 are concentrating among higher earners [9][2].

Possibility, not verified It is possible that Barr's "scenarios" framing, which declines to say whether AI will widen or narrow inequality, works as an ideology-style belief that lowers the urgency of collective resistance, favoring an ideology reading over pure coordination failure, but this rests only on his stated uncertainty, and no evidence was found on how workers receive the framing, so the possibility is speculative and could be shown wrong by survey data on worker expectations about AI [9].
