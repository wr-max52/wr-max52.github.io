Leaked Documents Expose Membership Roster and Off-the-Record Sessions of Peter Thiel's Dialog Society

A leaked registration list and internal database for the invitation-only organization reveal a convergence of government, corporate, and cultural figures, though the records do not substantiate claims of criminal conspiracy.

Corruption Published November 18, 2026 · 4 sources

What the Leak Reveals

Possibility, not verified It is possible that Dialog, the private society co-founded by Peter Thiel and Auren Hoffman, facilitates opaque influence networks between powerful figures in politics, tech, and finance, but available evidence does not substantiate claims of coordinated illegal activity or criminal conspiracy [1][2]. The organization, described as a private, invitation-only group cofounded in 2006 by billionaire tech investor Peter Thiel, has drawn public attention after a leak of its internal records [1][3].

The Leaked Documents

A leaked registration list for Dialog's 2026 retreat names 222 people and records each registrant's membership status and attendee type, including "active member" and "guest" [1]. The leaked documents show a program of off-the-record sessions including topics such as "Money (Does?) Buy Happiness," "Bring Back Nuclear," "Navigating WWIII," "Battlefield Technologies," and "How's Your Sex Life?" [1]. The records are stored in Airtable, a commercial database, and for each participant, Dialog logs a membership status, every retreat the person has attended, a biography, a home city, and a private access token functioning as a login credential [1]. Among the names is General Alexus Grynkewich, NATO's supreme allied commander Europe and head of US European Command, who took the post in July 2025 and is recorded as having attended Dialog gatherings since 2021 [1].

Membership and Structure

The organization has been compared to Bilderberg and the World Economic Forum, and since its founding there have been around 2,500 participants in annual events, among them over 1,000 paying members [3]. According to leaked information, Dialog staff and their artificial intelligence tools create dossiers on every member and potential member, and assign them a grade reflecting their desirability to Dialog: a C is given to the most famous and influential, A is assigned to established members who are less notable, while most people receive a B [3]. The list indicates an organization capable of mustering a unique mix of government, corporate and cultural power, though many names on the list probably attended Dialog events before Thiel's association with the political right and the Trump era [2].

Unverified Possibilities

Possibility, not verified It is possible that Dialog facilitates opaque influence networks between powerful figures in politics, tech, and finance, given the convergence of government officials, tech executives, and financiers at off-the-record retreats [1][2]. Possibility, not verified It is possible that the organization has conflicts of interest where officials overseeing industries meet with leaders of companies in those industries in private settings, as the leaked list includes figures like Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent alongside data-broker executives and Senator Ted Cruz alongside surveillance company founders [1]. Possibility, not verified It is possible that the organization's matchmaking service, which pairs single members through algorithmic tools, creates additional channels for influence and relationship-building beyond the formal retreat agenda [1][3]. Possibility, not verified It is possible that the leaked documents' agenda topics, including "Build-a-Cult" and "Navigating WWIII," reflect genuine discussions about controversial or extreme scenarios, though the documents do not show evidence of coordinated illegal activity or criminal conspiracy [1].

Limitations of the Evidence

The evidence does not yet establish that Dialog coordinates illegal activity, directs public policy, fixes government contracts, acts as a unified ideological organization, or requires members to follow Peter Thiel's instructions [4]. It does not establish that every person in the leaked records knowingly joined a "secret society," as some may have attended once, been invited, registered as guests, or appeared without understanding the organization in those terms [4]. Some names appear as guests, speakers, applicants, registrants, or past attendees, and appearance in a directory is not proof of conspiracy or misconduct [4].
