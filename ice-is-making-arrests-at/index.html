Possible Conflict at Boeing Field: ICE Arrests and Washington Sheriffs Ending 287(g) Agreements?

No source in the record confirms any arrest or any termination, but the structural facts leave open a question about federal and local enforcement colliding at a county-owned airport.

A County Airport, a Federal Question

Five miles south of downtown Seattle, King County owns and operates a public airport called Boeing Field [1]. The Metropolitan King County Council, as the county's legislative branch, "sets policies, enacts laws, and adopts budgets that guide an array of services" [3][4]. That is the structure behind a question no source in the record answers [1][3][4].

What the Record Actually Shows

That is institutional description. It references no ICE operation at any airport and no local cooperation agreement [2].

Possibility, not verified It is possible that federal enforcement at a county-owned airport and local withdrawal together form a growing pattern of jurisdictional conflict across Washington State [1][5][2]. The Case Against

No source documents an ICE arrest at Boeing Field, a sheriff terminating a 287(g) agreement, or a pattern of local withdrawal from federal cooperation [3][4][2][5][1]. The five sources confirm only structural facts: Boeing Field exists as a King County asset [1], the County Council sets policy [3][4], and federal agencies exist [2][5]. None of them records a single arrest, a single termination, or a single jurisdiction ending an agreement.

The Department of Justice describes its own structure: "The Department of Justice consists of multiple components and agencies dedicated to upholding our mission" [2]. That sentence names no airport, no arrest, and no contested jurisdiction [2]. A skeptic would call the thesis entirely speculative, built on structural inference rather than documented events [2][1].

What the Sources Do Not Establish

The five sources confirm three structural facts: King County owns Boeing Field as a public airport [1], the County Council sets policy and enacts laws for county services [3][4], and federal agencies operate under separate authority [2][5]. They stop there. No source records an arrest at the airport, a sheriff ending a 287(g) agreement, or a second jurisdiction withdrawing from federal cooperation [3][4][2][5][1].

The evidence leaves three questions open. Did ICE officers make arrests at Boeing Field in late 2026? Did any Washington State sheriff terminate a 287(g) agreement in 2026? Does more than one jurisdiction show the same move? The sources answer none of these [3][4][2][5][1]. What remains is structural inference, not documented event [3][4][2][5][1].
