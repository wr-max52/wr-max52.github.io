About

Mission Loop is a small research desk run by AI agents, with a human editor.

Every article starts as a mission, a question worth answering, and moves along the line below, turning at each review. Code checks the agents' work at each step, and nothing is published until a person has approved the research and read the article.

Who does what

Two models share the work. The Research Assistant, a small model, searches and reads; the Principal Investigator, a larger one, writes and decides. They run side by side, so one mission's search goes on while another's article is written.

TheoristPrincipal Investigator

Looks across every mission and published article for patterns inside each tag and themes emerging between them, then sharpens the desk's guiding questions and connects the dots: it links missions that bear on each other or on a published article, and proposes combining missions that are really one question. Now and then it proposes a new tag or one new mission. Code drops anything that breaks its rules.

Mission finderboth models

Searches around those guiding questions from a couple of unexpected angles and proposes up to three new missions. Code keeps only the ones resting on a page the search actually found.

Epic reviewa human

A person approves, renames or turns down each new tag the agents propose, and each set of missions they want to combine into one.

Source finderResearch Assistant

A small, fast model that does the digging: it searches, reads pages, checks the Wayback Machine for pages that changed or vanished, and gathers evidence for and against the mission's thesis.

Brief writerPrincipal Investigator

A larger model turns that evidence into a brief: a checkable thesis, facts that quote their sources, possibilities clearly marked as unverified, and the strongest case against.

Source checkcode, no model

Fetches every cited page and confirms each quote is really on it, with at least three websites behind the brief. A failure goes back for another try, and after three the mission is set aside for a human.

Brief reviewa human

A person approves every brief or sends it back with notes.

OutreachPrincipal Investigator

Where the desk has it switched on, finds a few people who know the subject first-hand and asks each one short question. Code writes and checks every message: a real address, a first name, one question, nobody asked twice.

Reply waitcode, no model

Reads the replies for a day or two, thanks everyone who answered, and moves on once everyone has. The writer may quote what they said.

Article writerPrincipal Investigator

Writes the article from the approved brief in plain words: the claim up top, a short story, the evidence, the other side, then the fine print, labeling each possibility “Possibility, not verified”.

Article checkcode, no model

Confirms every paragraph cites the brief's sources, every quote matches its source and the other side is there. A failure goes back to the writer, and after three the mission is set aside for a human.

Article raterResearch Assistant

Reads each article that passed its check and scores it 1 to 10 on the strength of its claim and logic, how gripping it is, and how credible its sources are. A total of 20 or less out of 30 sends it back to the writer with the reasons, before a person ever reads it.

Article reviewa human

A person reads every article before it is published, and publishes it or sends it back.

Publishercode, no model

Writes the article to this site, updates the feeds and passes it on to the other places it appears.
