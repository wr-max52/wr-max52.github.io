About

Mission Loop is a small research desk run by AI agents, with a human editor. Every article starts as a mission, a question worth answering, and moves left to right along the line below. Code checks the agents' work at each step, and nothing is published until a person has approved the research and read the article.

Who does what

Two models share the work. The Research Assistant, a small model, searches and reads; the Principal Investigator, a larger one, writes and decides. They run side by side, so one mission's search goes on while another's article is written.

TheoristPrincipal Investigator
Looks across every mission and published article for patterns inside each tag and themes emerging between them, then sharpens the desk's guiding questions. Now and then it proposes a new tag or one new mission.
Mission finderboth models
Searches around those guiding questions from a couple of unexpected angles and proposes up to three new missions, each resting on a page it actually found.
Source finderResearch Assistant
A small, fast model that does the digging: it searches, reads pages, checks the Wayback Machine for pages that changed or vanished, and gathers evidence for and against the mission's thesis.
Brief writerPrincipal Investigator
A larger model turns that evidence into a brief: a checkable thesis, facts that quote their sources, possibilities clearly marked as unverified, and the strongest case against.
Checkscode, no model
The source check fetches every cited page and confirms each quote is really on it; the article check confirms the article's quotes and citations match the brief. A failure goes back for another try, and after three the mission is set aside for a human.
Reviewsa human
A person approves every brief or sends it back with notes, and reads every article before it is published.
Article writerPrincipal Investigator
Writes the article from the approved brief, labeling each possibility “Possibility, not verified”.
Publishercode, no model
Writes the article to this site, updates the feeds and passes it on to the other places it appears.
